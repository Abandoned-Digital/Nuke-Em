How to Play

Each player rules a continent. You stockpile weapons, spread propaganda and launch at your neighbours, all while the Doomsday Clock ticks toward midnight. When it strikes, whoever has the most people left rules over the biggest pile of ash.

30Minutes to midnight
6Actions

01

Overview & winning

Every player rules one continent, made up of 3 to 6 cities. Each city holds population cubes. Nukes remove them, propaganda steals them.

Elimination: the dead hand

When your continent drops to 0 Population, you are eliminated, but not quietly. Right away you fire your whole remaining arsenal: every weapon in your hand and on your Launchpad and Runway, one after another, each at a continent and city of your choice. These are normal attacks: the targets may raise shields against them, and every nuke moves the Clock. Then you are out, and play goes on without you.

A dead hand can eliminate someone else, who then fires their own arsenal. Chain reactions are possible.

The game ends when

  • the Doomsday Clock reaches midnight: finish the current round, then the surviving player with the most Population wins;
  • only one continent is left: its player wins;
  • no continent is left: the Earth is destroyed and everybody loses.

03

Setup

  1. Lay out the game boardPut the world map in the middle of the table, where everyone can reach it.
  2. Take a continentTake its player board and put it in front of you. On the world map, fill every solid square of your continent's cities with population cubes of your colour; the dashed rows stay empty. Put your tracker cube on Pass. Your continent's special trait is printed on your player board and applies all game.
  3. Shuffle the decksShuffle the Build and Propaganda decks separately and put each face down on its spot on the game board. Each discard pile lies face up next to the board, beside its deck. Whenever a deck runs out, during setup too, shuffle its discard pile and put it face down as the new deck.
  4. Deal handsDeal each player 3 Build cards. You may put an upgrade among them into your Upgrade slot (see Build).
  5. Set the ClockPut a marker on the Clock: 5 players start at 32, 4 players at 30, 3 players at 27, 2 players at 22. Fewer players launch fewer nukes, so the fuse is shorter.
  6. First playerGive the first player token to the most paranoid player.

04

A round

  1. Turns, one by oneStarting with the first player and going clockwise, each player takes a turn: move your tracker cube to an action (not the one it is on, unless it is on Pass) and resolve it fully. You may play one Operation, before or after your action. Then the next player.
  2. End of roundDiscard the shields in your Shield slot. Pass the first player token clockwise.

Hand limit: 5, at all times

You never hold more than 5 Build and Propaganda cards (your tracker cube doesn't count). Whenever a card would be your sixth, choose any one of the six and discard it, the new card included. There is no discarding at the end of the round.

05

The six actions

BuildDraw 2 Build cards
PropagandaDraw 1 Propaganda card
PrepareLoad weapons face down
AttackFire everything loaded
IntelLook at a hand, draw 1
PassDo nothing

Never the same action twice in a row. Your tracker cube sits on the action you took last. On your turn you move it to the action you take now, and it may not stay where it is. Everyone can see what you can't do: if your cube is on Attack, you can't Attack this turn. The cube starts on Pass, and Pass is never locked (see Pass below).

Build

Draw 2 Build cards into your hand.

Empty deck? When the Build or Propaganda deck runs out, at any moment (even halfway through a draw), shuffle its discard pile into a new face-down deck and keep drawing. Cards still on the old deck stay on top.

Operations (green Build cards) don't cost an action, but you may play only one Operation per turn, before or after your action. Tricks from the Propaganda deck that you play on your turn don't count towards it.

Upgrades (purple Build cards) may go into an Upgrade slot as soon as you draw them; one you don't slot is discarded. You have two. If both are taken, or you draw more upgrades at once than you have room for, keep the ones you want and discard the others. Upgrades don't count toward your hand.

Propaganda

Choose an opponent as your target and draw 1 Propaganda card.

  • Success: take population cubes from one of the target's cities and put them on one of your cities. You choose both. If that city has fewer population cubes than the card takes, you get what it has and it becomes a crater. Craters can't give or receive population cubes.
  • Backfire: it goes wrong. When your population cubes defect, the target chooses which of your cities they leave.
  • Trick: keep it in your hand without showing it, and play it when the card says.

Gained population cubes go to the city of your choice, into its dashed row on the world map. A city can hold at most twice its starting population cubes, minus any spots lost to fallout; when it is full, pick another city. If all your cities are full, you can't take in more people: population cubes you would steal stay where they are, and those in the supply stay there. Defectors keep their colour and count for whoever's city they live in, so a nuke on that city kills them too.

Counter-Propaganda and Censorship answer any Propaganda card another player draws against you, Tricks included. Censorship resolves the card as if you had drawn it, aimed at the player who drew it; a Censored Trick goes to your hand. Olympic Gold targets every opponent, so each of them may answer it for their own population cube.

Propaganda never moves the Doomsday Clock.

Attack

Fire everything you have loaded: first the weapon on your Launchpad, then the one on your Runway. Each is its own attack: for each, choose an opponent's continent, then name a city on it. The two may go at different players. The details are in Attacks below. You can only choose Attack with something loaded (or a Nuclear Sub in hand: it is never loaded, and you may launch it from your hand along with the rest).

Everyone can see that you are loaded, but not with what, and you don't know whether your target holds a shield.

Prepare

Load weapons from your hand face down: a missile on your Launchpad, an aircraft on your Runway, or one of each. A slot holds one card, and it stays loaded until you Attack. Every weapon says on its card which slot it goes on (Launchpad or Runway); the Nuclear Sub goes on neither. You can only choose Prepare with a weapon for an empty slot. Loaded cards don't count towards your hand limit.

Since you can't take the same action twice in a row, a strike takes two turns: Prepare, then Attack.

Intel

Choose another player and look at their hand and at what is loaded on their Launchpad and Runway, then draw 1 Build card. Knowing who holds a shield (or what is coming) tells you where to strike.

Pass

Do nothing this round. You move your cube to Pass like any other action. But Pass is never locked: with your cube on Pass you may Pass again, and every other action is open. So you can Pass two rounds in a row, and after a Pass you choose from all six. Use it when nothing else is worth it, or to get every action back.

Shields

There is no Defend action. Shields are reactions: you keep them in your hand and play one when one of your cities is attacked (see Attacks). Playing a shield costs no action.

06

Attacks

  1. LaunchTurn over the weapon on your Launchpad, choose an opponent's continent and name any city on it that still has population cubes: "Europe, Paris!" The city numbers on the map are only there for quick reference. Anyone holding Deepfake may now change the continent.
  2. The target reactsThe target sees the weapon and the city, and decides: let it happen, or play one shield from their hand. An Iron Curtain already up blocks it without a new card. Then any other player may play an Interceptor to block it.
  3. ResolveThe weapon hits the city you named. Nobody can change it unless a card says so. If it was blocked, nothing happens. Otherwise remove the weapon's damage in population cubes. Overkill is wasted.
  4. Move the ClockMove it forward by the weapon's Clock value, even if the attack was blocked or hit nothing.
  5. DiscardDiscard the weapon. A Nuclear Sub in the target's hand may now strike straight back at you.
  6. NextThen the same for the weapon on your Runway, as a separate attack (at the same or another player).

Craters

  • A city with no population cubes left is a crater: it can never hold population cubes again. No token needed, the empty city box says it all. That includes a city your own Civil Defence empties.
  • You can't aim at a crater. Name a city that still has population cubes.

Fallout

A nuke is any weapon with a Clock value of +1 or more. When a nuke removes population cubes from a city, put one fallout token for every population cube it removed on that city's rightmost empty spots: an ICBM that removes 5 leaves 5, a Tactical Nuke that removes 3 leaves 3. Those spots can never hold a population cube again. The bigger the bomb, the longer the mess lasts. A MIRV leaves 2 on every city it removes 2 population cubes from; the Fallout Bomb also puts 2 fallout on every other city of that continent; the Dirty Bomb leaves 3. Fallout that doesn't fit on the empty spots is lost.

Read a city box as one line of spots, solid row first, then dashed. Population cubes always fill from the left and fallout always fills from the right, so the first hits on a city take away its room to grow (the dashed row), and once that is gone, fallout eats into the starting spots. A city whose spots are all fallout is gone for good. Conventional weapons (Clock +0) leave no fallout. Reconstruction can remove 1 fallout.

Shields

Every shield blocks an attack completely, and you choose it knowing what is coming. Shields differ in what they block: one attack, every attack on you this round, or one attack sent straight back. See the Build Deck tab. A played shield lies in your Shield slot until the end of the round, then goes to the discard pile. Bunker Buster and Hypersonic Nuke can't be blocked by any shield: no Missile Defence, no Interceptor, not even an Iron Curtain already up. Only Civil Defence still helps, because it doesn't block. Radar Blackout takes a player's shields down and stops them raising any (Civil Defence included) for the rest of the round; another player's Interceptor can still save them. An attack sent back by Return to Sender can be blocked by the attacker's own Iron Curtain, but it can't be sent back a second time.

The guessing game

The attacker never knows whether you hold a shield, and you never know what is coming until it is in the air. A shield in your hand is a weapon you don't hold: the hand limit is 5.

07

Example: one attack

You play Europe. Your player board has the action track, a Launchpad, a Runway, a Shield slot and two Upgrade slots. Last turn you chose Prepare and loaded an ICBM face down on your Launchpad, so your tracker cube is on Prepare. North America holds a Missile Defence.

  1. Your turnYou move your tracker cube from Prepare to Attack, turn over the ICBM on your Launchpad and name the target: "North America, New York!"
  2. North America reactsAn ICBM takes 5 population cubes, so North America plays its Missile Defence into its Shield slot. The attack is blocked. The Clock still moves +2. Discard the ICBM.
  3. If they had let it happenNew York loses 5 population cubes and gets 5 fallout, and the Clock moves +2.
  4. End of roundNorth America discards its Missile Defence. Your cube stays on Attack: you can't Attack on your next turn, and everyone can see that.
Saving a shield

A shield is spent once it blocks. North America might rather let a 1-damage Airstrike through and keep its Missile Defence for the ICBM it expects next.

08

The Doomsday Clock

The Clock counts minutes to midnight. It only moves when weapons are launched: each one moves it by its Clock value. No nukes, no midnight.

About half an hour to midnight. Starting positions: 32 for 5 players · 30 for 4 · 27 for 3 · 22 for 2. Red square is midnight.

Some Build cards turn the Clock back, such as Peace Treaty (5 minutes). The Clock never goes above its starting position.

Conventional weapons have Clock +0. A player who is behind wants a long game and fights with them. The leader wants the game over and reaches for nukes.

A nuke sent back by Return to Sender does not move the Clock again. It's the same bomb.

09

Scoring

Count every population cube on your cities, whatever its colour. Most wins.

Tie: the player with the most surviving cities (not craters) wins. If still tied, share the victory and the radiation.

Releases

0.15.2

Optional upgrades

  • Fallout Shelters is now Bomb Shelters: Once per round, when a nuke hits your cities, it removes 1 population cube fewer from each of them. Cancelling fallout almost never mattered (fallout only takes the room a city has to grow), so the card did next to nothing in simulation; the new one is worth about as much as Early Warning or Spin Doctors.
  • Shield Network is gone; Mobile Launchers takes its place: When you Attack, you may also fire one weapon from your hand. A shield that came back every round made its owner nearly untouchable when attacked once a round. Mobile Launchers lets you strike without loading first: worth little at a crowded table, a lot in a duel.
  • Slotting an upgrade is optional. Every upgrade now reads When you draw this, you may put it in your Upgrade slot. An upgrade you don't slot is discarded. Upgrades already in your slots stay unless a new one needs the room.

0.15.1

Trait check

  • North America: Arsenal of Democracy replaces American Exceptionalism: When you Prepare, also draw 1 Build card. Since 0.15 every strike needs a Prepare first, so taking the same action twice in a row hardly ever helped: North America won 2 to 5 points under its fair share in simulation and the trait was worth about 1 point. With the new trait it lands near fair at every player count (52% / 35% / 25% / 19% for 2 to 5 players). North America no longer repeats actions: its tracker cube moves like everyone else's.
  • South America: Commodity Boom now reads When you Build, draw 3 Build cards instead of 2. (it used to draw 3 and keep 2). In simulation South America won clearly less than its fair share (about 15% of 5-player games, fair is 20%); drawing 3 and keeping all of them brings it close to fair at every player count (49.5% / 32% / 22–25% / 19% for 2 to 5 players). Asia was checked too and is on target: its trait stays as it is.
  • Europe: Old Diplomacy replaces Intelligence Services: Propaganda against you takes at most 1 population cube. Looking at an attacker's hand before blocking was worth almost nothing in simulation. The new trait blunts the big Propaganda steals and leaves Build and Intel as they are for everyone. Simulated win rates: 52% / 37% / 28% / 23% for 2 to 5 players, slightly above fair.

0.15.0

Loaded and ready

  • Prepare is back, in place of Relief. Load weapons face down from your hand: a missile on your Launchpad, an aircraft on your Runway, or one of each. They stay loaded until you Attack. The delay is back, but shields still answer at once, and there are no launch codes or fizzles.
  • Attack fires everything loaded, the Launchpad first, then the Runway. Each is its own attack and may go at a different player. The Nuclear Sub is never loaded: it goes from your hand, with Attack or as a strike back.
  • Every weapon names its slot on the card: Launchpad (Tactical Nuke, ICBM, MIRV, Fallout Bomb, Tsar Bomba, Hypersonic Nuke, Dirty Bomb) or Runway (Airstrike, Nuclear Bomber, Bunker Buster, Carpet Bombing). A Nuclear Bomber that turns back lands on the Runway again.
  • No more Relief. Damage now stays: the winning score drops and more cities end as craters.
  • Extra Silo and Alpha Strike are gone: two loaded slots do their job.
  • Missile Recall is now Abort Mission: another player's Launchpad and Runway go back to their hand.
  • Sabotage, Disarmament Talks, Leaked Launch Codes and Intel reach the loaded slots too.
  • Player boards: a Launchpad and a Runway next to the action track. The tracker cube shows everyone your last action; there is no Action slot.
  • In simulation (4 players): games run about 29 rounds and end at midnight 9 times in 10; about half of all attacks are blocked; the winning score is about 12 and knockouts happen in most games (0.9 a game).

0.14.0

Straight from the hand

  • No more Prepare and Defend. Four actions: Build, Propaganda, Attack, Pass. Attack launches a weapon straight from your hand. You still can't Attack twice in a row.
  • Shields are reactions. When one of your cities is attacked, you see the weapon and the city, then choose: let it happen, or play one shield from your hand. The attacker never knows whether you hold one. A Defend that waited for an attack that never came, and the attacker who switched targets after seeing a Defend, are both gone.
  • No Launchpad, so no fizzling. Cards that lived on it have new texts: Nuclear Sub (launch it with Attack, or right after someone attacks you, back at them), Missile Recall (a weapon from the discard pile), Disarmament Talks (everyone scraps a weapon), Sabotage (destroy a weapon or shield in a hand), Spy Satellite (everyone's shield count), Leaked Launch Codes (everyone's weapons), Whistleblower (show one card from a hand), Extra Silo (Attack launches 1 more), Early Warning (a card when attacked), Shield Network (a shield that blocked comes back).
  • No planning phase. Players take their turns one by one: move your tracker cube to an action and resolve it, with one Operation before or after. With shields as reactions, the hidden plan had little left to hide.
  • Two new actions: Relief (2 population cubes from the supply on your cities, above the starting population too, never on fallout) and Intel (look at another player's hand, then draw 1 Build card). Six actions again.
  • Two Upgrade slots again, in the room the Launchpad left.
  • An action tracker instead of action cards. Each board has an action track: the six actions in a column, each with a square. Move your tracker cube to the action you take; it can't stay where it is (unless it is on Pass). The cube shows everyone what you can't take next. No Action or Locked slot, no action cards.
  • One Operation per turn. Five Operations in one turn (two Black Markets replaying an Arms Race and buying back a Tsar Bomba) was too much.
  • Europe: Intelligence Services: once per round, when another player attacks you, look at their hand before you block.
  • Every weapon hits 1 harder (Airstrike 2, Tactical Nuke 3, Nuclear Bomber 4, ICBM 5, Tsar Bomba 6; MIRV and Carpet Bombing 2 per city), so fallout grows with it. Too many games ended at midnight with most cities still standing. In simulation (4 players) knockouts triple and craters double; the game length stays the same, because the Clock sets it.
  • Missile Defence: 6 copies instead of 8.
  • Radar Blackout (1, Operation): discard the shields in another player's Shield slot and put the card there; they can't play shields for the rest of the round. Played early in the round, everyone after you can hit them too. The Build deck holds 69 cards (two Missile Defences fewer).
  • No more Five-Year Plan or Rewrite History. A deck that runs out is simply reshuffled from its discard pile, at any moment, during setup too. Drawing Five-Year Plan while dealing the opening hands meant shuffling before the game had begun. The Build deck holds 68 cards, the Propaganda deck 33.
  • Specials are now called Operations (the green Build cards played on your own turn). Same cards, new name.
  • In simulation (4 players), with everything above: games run about 25 rounds and nearly always end at midnight; about half of all attacks are blocked; Relief puts back about 33 population cubes a game, so the winning score is about 17 and knockouts are rare again (0.3 a game). Relief is the dial to watch in playtests.

0.13.0

Alpha Strike

  • Alpha Strike (1, special): play it on your turn to fire every weapon in your hand, one at a time, each resolved as normal (Clock, shields, Interceptor, Deepfake). Next round your action must be Pass. The Build deck grows to 70 cards.
  • Pass is never locked. It goes straight back into your hand at the end of the round, and your Locked card still comes back: after a Pass nothing is locked, and you can Pass two rounds in a row.

0.12.0

Bigger deck, sharper news

  • Five new specials (8 cards): Weather Balloon (2, look at a hand), Codebreakers (2, look at the top 3 Build cards, take 1, and put the other 2 back on top or discard them), War Bonds (2, draw 1 plus 1 for each of your damaged cities), Missile Recall (take a prepared card back) and Disarmament Talks (every Launchpad is emptied, the Clock goes back 1). The Build deck grows to 69 cards.
  • Four new Propaganda cards (5 cards): State TV Blooper (2) backfires with a cube, Bread Lines with 2, Exposed Spy Ring backfires with a card, Defecting Scientist wins you a card. Propaganda was the strongest action; with more Backfires it is a real gamble again.
  • Olympic Gold only takes from opponents who have more population than you: a catch-up card instead of the strongest card in the deck.
  • Doomsday Bunker adds 2 at midnight instead of 3.
  • Card sheets: Build 1 now holds cards 1–36 (all weapons and every shield but Civil Defence), Build 2 cards 37–69, Propaganda cards 1–34.

0.11.0

No bluffing, fewer cities

  • No more decoys. Cardboard Missile, Inflatable Silo, Firework and Weather Balloon leave the deck. Prepare only takes a weapon.
  • SLBM is now the Nuclear Sub (2): hits for 3 and never fizzles; it waits on your Launchpad until you launch it, or until you Prepare another card in its place. The Nuclear Sub upgrade is gone: nobody has to track how long a card has waited.
  • New: Black Market (2): take the top card of the Build discard pile, or draw 1 Build card if it is empty.
  • A second Reconstruction, Arms Deal and Arms Race. Arms Race now gives you 2 Build cards and everyone else 1.
  • One city fewer per continent: Vancouver, Madrid, Mumbai, Kinshasa and Lima are gone, and every continent starts with 16 population cubes (Berlin 3, Tokyo 8). City numbers follow the new map. In the simulations a continent is knocked out in about 1 game in 6 instead of 1 in 30.
  • Build deck: 61 cards on two sheets (Build 1: cards 1–29, Build 2: cards 30–61).

0.10.1

One of each

  • One copy of each upgrade and one Five-Year Plan: the Build deck drops from 72 to 64 cards. Take the second copies out of the deck.
  • Five-Year Plan works when you draw it: shuffle it and the Build discard pile into the Build deck, then draw 2 Build cards. It never stays in a hand, so the Build deck can't run out.

0.10.0

Clean table

  • Five players: South America joins (Bogotá, Lima, São Paulo, Buenos Aires; 20 Population) with Commodity Boom: when it Builds, it draws 3 cards and keeps 2. With 5 players the Clock starts at 32.
  • No more launch codes. Prepare puts only the card face down; Attack chooses the continent and the city at launch. The launch code cards are retired.
  • Europe: Intelligence Services looks at the card another player puts on their Launchpad when they Prepare (once per round). Spy Satellite shows all cards on one Launchpad. SLBM hits for 3 and looks like a decoy to anyone who peeks. Deepfake: when any weapon is launched, you pick the continent it goes to (not your own); the attacker names the city.
  • Pass is a sixth action card: do nothing this round. It counts for the Locked slot like any other action.
  • Previous Action is now called Locked. The cleanup step is simply the end of the round: shields are discarded and the action cards move to Locked.
  • Hand limit 5, at all times: when a card would be your sixth, discard any one of the six. No discarding at the end of the round.
  • One Upgrade slot. Drawing an upgrade with the slot taken, or two at once, you keep one and discard the rest. Player boards have five slots in one row: Action, Locked, Launchpad, Shield, Upgrade.
  • All of these rules are the same in the digital game, which now plays like the table.

0.9.1

Eyes in the sky

  • Spy Satellite shows all cards on one player's Launchpad, launch code included, instead of one prepared card.

0.9.0

Simulated

  • Rules engine and simulator. Every rule and card is now played out by bots, thousands of games at a time; the open cases below were settled along the way.
  • New traits, balanced in simulation: Europe Intelligence Services looks at the launch code when another player Prepares (once per round). Asia Hardened Megacities: a nuke that would deal 3 or more damage to one of its cities deals 1 less. Africa Bounce Back: every time it loses a city, 1 population cube from the supply goes on another of its cities. North America is unchanged. Simulated win rates at 4 players: North America 23%, Europe 27%, Asia 25%, Africa 25% (World's Factory and Population Boom gave Asia 19% and Africa 40%).
  • Fallout equals the damage dealt: a nuke leaves one fallout for every population cube it removes. Fallout Shelters cancel all fallout on one city once per round.
  • Decoys pay at the end of the round they fizzle, counting other players' Defends revealed that round. A sabotaged decoy pays nothing.
  • Five-Year Plan and Rewrite History shuffle themselves back in with their discard pile, so a deck practically never runs dry.
  • Nuclear Bomber turning back returns to your hand.
  • Spin Doctors draws 1 extra Propaganda card and resolves both, one after another.
  • Counter-Propaganda and Censorship answer any Propaganda card another player draws against you, Tricks included. Censorship resolves the card as if you had drawn it, aimed at them. Each opponent may answer Olympic Gold for their own cube.
  • Hacked Guidance gives the weapon a new target on any continent, following that weapon's own rules.
  • Clarified: everyone plans at the same time, with no turn order (manual and Round helper card); a steal takes from one city (a short city craters); a volley's later weapons are wasted on a fresh crater; a dead launch code fizzles; a returned attack meets the attacker's shield but can't bounce again; shield-ignoring weapons ignore Interceptor; Civil Defence may empty a city; Attack with only a decoy is allowed; the cube supply is unlimited.

0.8.7

Name your target

  • Prepare aims at a continent, Attack names the city. Prepare puts your weapon and one launch code (that opponent's card) face down; when you launch you simply name any city on that continent that still has population cubes. Picking city cards took too long at the table (first playtest).
  • No more city cards: each player keeps 3 launch codes, one per opponent (12 cards instead of 35). City numbers are only for quick reference; nothing in the game counts them. Wrong codes and misfires are gone; you can't aim at a crater.
  • No supply boxes on the world map: the population cube and fallout boxes are gone. In the playtest everyone kept their own cubes beside them anyway.
  • One more copy of every shield (Missile Defence 8, Iron Curtain 3, Return to Sender 2, Interceptor 2, Civil Defence 3): 18 shields instead of 13, Build deck 72 cards. In the first playtest everyone had plenty of nukes but little defence.
  • The Clock no longer ticks every round. Only launched weapons move it, by their Clock value. In the first playtest (3 players) the passive minute ended the game long before anyone could be wiped out, so a sole survivor was impossible.
  • Fallout equals the Clock value: a nuke leaves as many fallout as its Clock value, so a hit city also loses its room to grow. With 1 fallout per hit it hardly mattered, since most cities fall in one or two hits (first playtest). Dirty Bomb still leaves 3; Fallout Shelters cancel 1 of it.
  • Card texts without city numbers: Hacked Guidance sends the missile to another city of the defender's choice. Leaked Launch Codes and Early Warning show all cards on one player's Launchpad. Return to Sender hits a city of your choice on the attacker's continent. Fallout Bomb hits for 2 and puts 2 fallout on every other city of that continent. Carpet Bombing hits two cities of your choice for 1 each. SLBM may switch to another opponent's continent at launch.

0.8.6

Reading order

  • Defend always reacts to the first attack: a face-down Defend must be revealed as soon as you are attacked, and a raised shield always takes the first attack that comes in. No more holding a shield back for a bigger attack later.
  • North America: American Exceptionalism replaces Second Strike, to keep things simple: North America may play the same action two rounds in a row. Its board has no Previous Action slot: at cleanup it takes its action card straight back into its hand, so picking it up never gives away its plan.
  • One world map replaces the game board and the maps on the player boards: all four continents and their cities on one A2 board in the middle of the table, so everyone can see every city's population cubes when picking a target. The Clock, both decks, the cube supply and the fallout sit in its empty oceans.
  • Player boards are now just the continent name, its trait and the six card slots in one row, two boards per A3 sheet.
  • City numbers follow the map, top left to bottom right: North America 1 Vancouver, 2 New York, 3 Los Angeles, 4 Mexico City. Europe 1 London, 2 Berlin, 3 Paris, 4 Moscow, 5 Madrid, 6 Rome. Asia 1 Tokyo, 2 Shanghai, 3 Mumbai. Africa 1 Cairo, 2 Lagos, 3 Kinshasa, 4 Nairobi, 5 Johannesburg. Launch code cards and the Targets helper cards are updated to match.

0.8.5

No encores

  • Never the same action twice in a row. Your last action card lies face up in the new Previous Action slot and can't be planned this round. During cleanup it goes back to your hand and this round's card takes its place.
  • Up to 2 upgrades per player again, to make room on the board.
  • Board slots in three rows: Action and Previous Action, Launchpad and Shield, Upgrade 1 and Upgrade 2.

0.8.4

Spies and second strikes

  • New continent traits, so each continent plays in its own field: North America (retaliation), Europe (information), Asia (cards), Africa (population).
  • North America: Second Strike replaces Hollywood. When a nuke takes population cubes from North America and its Launchpad is empty, it may Prepare right away, out of turn. That card launches next round like any other, or fizzles.
  • Shields stay in the Shield slot until the end of the round, even once used up, and are discarded during cleanup. Missile Defence and Civil Defence lose their "then discard it".
  • No crater tokens: a city with no population cubes left is a crater, and the empty city box shows it.
  • Smaller game board (303 × 188 mm): the discard spots and the craters box are gone. The cube supply sits under the Build deck, the fallout tokens under the Propaganda deck. Discard piles lie face up next to the board.
  • Europe: Intelligence Services replaces Free Press. After everyone has planned, Europe looks at one other player's face-down action card.
  • Wording: the cubes on cities are always called population cubes, on cards, boards, helper cards and in this manual.

0.8.3

Slots and silos

  • Board slots: Launchpad, Action and Shield in the first column, three Upgrade slots in the second. The Second Silo slot is gone.
  • Up to 3 upgrades per player. Every upgrade is in the Build deck twice (67 cards). Two copies of the same upgrade add up.
  • Second Silo is now Extra Silo: Prepare may put 1 more card on your Launchpad, so two Extra Silos let you hold 3. All of them fire at the one target as separate attacks.
  • Fizzled cards go to the discard face up. The discard pile is open, and it proves the card was a real weapon or decoy. Sabotage follows the same rule.
  • Prepare: your card and your code cards go down as one face-down stack; the order doesn't matter.
  • Setup starts by putting the game board in the middle; the decks go on their spots on the board, and a marker goes on the Clock.
  • The Final Exchange variant is dropped for now.
  • Every upgrade stacks with its second copy. Early Warning's look is optional. Fallout Shelters now cancels 1 fallout per round on one of your cities (it no longer softens hits). Propaganda HQ is renamed Spin Doctors: draw 1 extra Propaganda card and keep one. Shield Network lets Defend play 1 extra shield card, so each extra block still costs a card.

0.8.2

Code cards

  • Launch codes are cards instead of 16 mm tokens: easier to pick, hold and stack. 3 continent cards and up to 6 city cards per player, one sheet for all four players.
  • Each city card lists only the real cities its number hits on your opponents' continents; each continent card lists that continent's cities. Europe gets no City 6 card, since no opponent has a sixth city.
  • Build card names fit at 11 pt: Propaganda Ministry is now Propaganda HQ, Early Warning Radar Early Warning, Disarmament Treaty Peace Treaty, Hypersonic Missile Hypersonic Nuke, Nuclear Submarine Nuclear Sub, Big Firework Firework. Second Silo and Propaganda HQ got clearer rules text.
  • Build card texts reviewed for clarity. Rule fixes along the way: Fallout Bomb and Carpet Bombing say a missing neighbour isn't hit; Return to Sender fizzles if the attacker has no city with that number; the Nuclear Bomber turning back doesn't move the Clock; Hypersonic Nuke simply ignores shields, like the Bunker Buster; upgrades go in an Upgrade slot.
  • Fallout is a general rule now: every nuke that removes cubes puts a fallout token on the city's rightmost empty spot, which can never hold a cube again. It eats the dashed row first, then the starting spots. Dirty Bomb leaves 3; Reconstruction can remove 1.
  • A shared game board: the Doomsday Clock in the middle, spots for both decks and their discards, and boxes for craters, fallout and the cube supply.
  • Attack: flip the launch code and the weapon together; the order doesn't matter.
  • Action cards are printed: 5 per player in their colour, with one shared back per player.
  • Second Silo fires its two weapons as two separate attacks at the same target (one Missile Defence stops only one).
  • A second Five-Year Plan joins the Build deck (60 cards), so an empty Build deck is less likely.
  • Kept Propaganda tricks stay hidden in your hand.
  • Arms Deal: you choose the card you give; the card you take is still random.
  • Nuclear Bomber turning back may re-aim: take the code back and lay a new one face down (or the same). It still has to launch next round or it fizzles.
  • Tsar Bomba hits for 5 instead of 6 (50 megatons); it is still the strongest single-target weapon. A few nukes now name their yield in the lore.
  • SLBM is now prepared with a launch code like any other card (a launchpad without a code gave it away); when it launches you may ignore the code and pick any city.
  • Decoys always pay: when one fizzles, draw 1 Build card, plus 1 more if anyone revealed Defend while it waited (Weather Balloon: plus 1 per defender). A decoy turn is now about as good as a Build turn instead of a gamble.
  • Hand limit 5 (was 6). It counts Build and Propaganda cards only, not action or launch code cards.
  • No more oceans. A code must name a real city. A wrong code by mistake is a misfire: the weapon fizzles without effect and can't be re-aimed.
  • The code cards lie face down on top of your prepared card, so the Launch Code spot on the boards is gone.
  • Helper cards reworked: no footer tag, aligned number badges, bullets beside headings, roomier Targets and fuller sentences.

0.8.1

Table aids

  • Helper cards: 4 double-sided poker cards per player: Round + Attack, Actions + Good to know, Targets (every continent's city numbers), Setup + Game end.
  • Launch code tokens are printed: 9 per player in their colour (3 opponent continents and city numbers 1–6), all with the same back.
  • The Launch Code spot on every board is bigger, with room for a continent token and a city token side by side.

0.8

Dead hand

  • Elimination no longer ends the game. A continent at 0 fires its whole remaining arsenal (launchpad and hand), then its player is out and play continues.
  • The game ends at midnight, when one continent is left (it wins), or when none is left: the Earth is destroyed and everybody loses.

0.7

Audit

  • Half an hour to midnight: the Clock starts at 30 (4 players), 27 (3) or 22 (2). Disarmament Treaty can turn it back 5 minutes.
  • When the Clock hits midnight or a continent hits 0, the current round still finishes; then the game ends.
  • Tie: most surviving cities wins.
  • Final Exchange (variant): every weapon still in hand fires, one after another in turn order, no shields.
  • Decoys pay off: draw a Build card if anyone reveals Defend while your decoy waits.
  • Prepare needs a weapon or decoy in hand; Attack needs something on your launchpad.
  • Europe (Free Press) also gains 1 cube when Propaganda against it backfires. Asia (World's Factory) draws 3 and keeps 2.
  • Five-Year Plan and Rewrite History reshuffle their decks, so a deck never runs dry for long.
  • 19 new Build cards (SLBM, Hypersonic Missile, Dirty Bomb, Carpet Bombing, Weather Balloon, Interceptor, Civil Defence, Disarmament Treaty, Arms Race, Sabotage, Five-Year Plan, and the upgrades Nuclear Submarine, Doomsday Bunker, Shield Network) and 11 new Propaganda cards.

0.6

Player board

  • The player board has six card slots: Launchpad (with a Code spot), Action, Second Silo, Shield and two Upgrade slots.
  • Each player can have at most 2 upgrades. Drawing a third, you keep the 2 you want.
  • The manual has a worked example of two rounds: Prepare then Attack, and Defending.
  • Every city on the board has a solid row (starting cubes) and a dashed row of the same size for gained cubes. A city holds at most twice its starting cubes. With every city full, you can't gain cubes at all.
  • The round summary left the board (it's in the manual, and can become a helper card).
  • The card slots sit in 2 columns of 3: Launchpad and Action, Second Silo and Upgrade 1, Shield and Upgrade 2. The map fills the rest of the board, which is now 406 × 240 mm.
  • All four player boards exist: North America, Europe, Asia and Africa, each with its own outline, cities, colour and trait.
  • 1 cube is about 5 million people, with each city standing for its metropolitan region (it used to say 100 million).

0.5

Open questions

  • The game ends immediately when the Clock hits midnight or a continent hits 0, even mid-round.
  • You may only plan Defend if you hold a shield. Bluff out loud, not with the cards.
  • Specials are played on your own turn, before or after your action, for free.
  • One action per round stays. 2-player games only differ in the Clock's starting position.
  • North America's city 4 is now Vancouver instead of Chicago, for a better spread across the continent.
  • Continent traits: North America Hollywood (Propaganda steals 1 extra cube), Europe Free Press (at most 1 cube stolen per card), Asia World's Factory (Build draws 3), Africa Population Boom (Build also adds 1 cube).

0.4

Propaganda

  • The Propaganda deck exists: 18 cards, half successes, a quarter backfires, the rest tricks you keep in hand.
  • Stolen cubes go straight onto one of your cities, in their owner's colour. The Defectors box is gone, and cities have no cap.
  • Propaganda never moves the Doomsday Clock.

0.3

Bluffs

  • Build draws 2 cards instead of 3.
  • Decoys are never launched. You prepare one to bait a Defend, then let it fizzle.
  • A prepared card that fizzles is discarded face down, so nobody learns whether it was real.
  • Early Warning Radar now looks at one prepared launch code at the start of each round.

One launchpad

  • Upgrades join the Build deck (purple): Second Silo (a second launchpad, never more than two), Early Warning Radar, Fallout Shelters, Propaganda Ministry. They take effect as soon as you draw them.
  • The target is chosen at Prepare: the launch code goes face down on top of the prepared card. Action cards are now planned without a code under them.
  • The Build deck grows to 40 cards over two print sheets. The deck is no longer limited to one sheet.

0.1

First skeleton

  • Doomsday Clock times the game: 1 minute per round plus each weapon's Clock value. Midnight ends the game.
  • Most Population wins. A continent at 0 ends the game at once, so nobody is eliminated and sits out.
  • Population is cubes on numbered cities. Continents keep different city counts (Asia 3, North America 4, Africa 5, Europe 6) with 20 Population each.
  • Rounds: everyone plans in secret, then each player in turn reveals and resolves, starting with the first player.
  • Launch codes commit an exact city (continent + number) during planning. Craters make the rubble bounce, missing numbers land in the ocean. Every player always places a code.
  • Shields fully block and cover the whole continent. Defend can interrupt out of turn when you're attacked.
  • Shield types: Missile Defence (one attack), Iron Curtain (all attacks this round), Return to Sender (sends it back, no extra Clock).
  • Eight weapon types, including spread damage (MIRV, Fallout Bomb), a shield-piercing Bunker Buster, a Bomber that can turn back, and a Clock-free conventional strike.
