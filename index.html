How to Play

Each player rules a continent. You stockpile weapons, spread propaganda and launch at your neighbours, all while the Doomsday Clock ticks toward midnight. When it strikes, whoever has the most people left rules over the biggest pile of ash.

01

Overview & winning

Every player rules one continent, made up of 3 to 6 cities. Each city holds Population cubes. Nukes remove cubes, propaganda steals them.

Elimination: the dead hand

When your continent drops to 0 Population, you are eliminated, but not quietly. Right away you fire your whole remaining arsenal: every weapon on your launchpad and in your hand, one after another, each at a continent and city of your choice. These are normal attacks: shields block them, targets may interrupt with Defend, and every nuke moves the Clock. Then you are out, and play goes on without you.

A dead hand can eliminate someone else, who then fires their own arsenal. Chain reactions are possible.

The game ends when

  • the Doomsday Clock reaches midnight: finish the current round, then the surviving player with the most Population wins;
  • only one continent is left: its player wins;
  • no continent is left: the Earth is destroyed and everybody loses.

03

Setup

  1. Lay out the game boardPut the game board in the middle of the table.
  2. Take a continentFill every solid square on your board with tracker cubes of your colour; the dashed rows stay empty. Take your 5 action cards and your launch code cards. Your continent's special trait is printed on its board and applies all game.
  3. Shuffle the decksShuffle the Build and Propaganda decks separately and put each face down on its spot on the game board, next to its discard spot.
  4. Deal handsDeal each player 3 Build cards.
  5. Set the ClockPut a marker on the Clock: 4 players start at 30, 3 players at 27, 2 players at 22. Fewer players launch fewer nukes, so the fuse is shorter.
  6. First playerGive the first player token to the most paranoid player.

04

A round

  1. Plan, in secretEveryone places one action card face down.
  2. Reveal and resolve, one by oneStarting with the first player and going clockwise, each player reveals their action and resolves it fully before the next player reveals.
  3. End of roundMove the Clock 1 minute toward midnight. Discard any shield in your Shield slot. Take your action card back into your hand. Discard down to 5 Build and Propaganda cards in hand (action cards and launch code cards don't count). Pass the first player token clockwise.

05

The five actions

BuildDraw 2 Build cards
PropagandaDraw 1 Propaganda card
Prepare1 card onto your launchpad
AttackLaunch it the next round

Build

Draw 2 Build cards into your hand.

Specials (green Build cards) are played on your own turn, before or after your action, and don't cost an action.

Upgrades (purple Build cards) go into an Upgrade slot on your board as soon as you draw them. You can have at most 3, one per Upgrade slot. If you draw a fourth, keep the 3 you want and discard the other. Upgrades add up: two copies of the same upgrade give its effect twice.

Propaganda

Choose an opponent as your target and draw 1 Propaganda card.

  • Success: take cubes from one of the target's cities and put them on one of your cities. You choose both. Craters can't give or receive cubes.
  • Backfire: it goes wrong. When your cubes defect, the target chooses which of your cities they leave.
  • Trick: keep it in your hand without showing it, and play it when the card says.

Gained cubes go to the city of your choice, into its dashed row on your board. A city can hold at most twice its starting cubes, minus any spots lost to fallout; when it is full, pick another city. If all your cities are full, you can't take in more people: cubes you would steal stay where they are, and cubes from the supply stay in the supply. Defectors keep their colour and count for whoever's city they live in, so a nuke on that city kills them too.

Propaganda never moves the Doomsday Clock.

Prepare

Put 1 card from your hand and your launch code (one continent card and one city card) face down on your Launchpad as one stack; the order doesn't matter. The code must name a city that exists on that continent; your city cards only list real targets. The card can be a real weapon or a decoy. Everyone sees that you have something ready, but not what, and not where it's going.

The code is locked in. Next round the board may have changed, but your missile still flies to the city you picked, unless a card says otherwise (SLBM, Nuclear Bomber, Hacked Guidance, Deepfake).

Your Launchpad holds 1 prepared card. Each Extra Silo upgrade lets Prepare put 1 more card on it, so with both copies it holds 3. All cards on your Launchpad share the one launch code.

Use it or lose it

A prepared card must be launched the very next round. If your next action is anything other than Attack, the card fizzles: put it on the discard pile face up when you reveal that action. The discard pile is open, so everyone sees it was a real weapon or a decoy. You can't sit on a missile and wait for someone to drop their guard.

Decoys

A decoy is never launched. You prepare it so the table thinks a missile is coming, hope someone wastes a Defend, then do something else and let it fizzle. It always pays a little: when it fizzles, draw 1 Build card, plus 1 more if anyone revealed Defend while it waited.

You can only plan Prepare if you have a weapon or decoy in hand, and only plan Attack if something is on your launchpad.

Attack

Launch what you prepared last round. The details are in Attacks below. An SLBM is prepared with a code like any weapon, so nobody can spot it, but when you launch it you may ignore the code and choose any city.

Defend

When your Defend is revealed, you must play a shield card from your hand. It covers your whole continent until the end of the round, or until it is used up. If nobody attacks you, it's wasted.

Interrupt: if your face-down action is Defend and someone attacks you, you may reveal it immediately, out of turn, right after their weapon is revealed. You know what's incoming, so you can pick the right shield. You then skip your Defend on your normal turn.

You may only plan Defend if you hold a shield card. Bluff out loud as much as you like, but the cards have to back you up.

06

Attacks

  1. Reveal the code and the weaponTurn over your launch code, for example Europe 3 (Paris), and your prepared card together; the order doesn't matter. With more than one card on your Launchpad, they all fire at the same target, each as a separate attack: one Missile Defence stops only one of them.
  2. The target may interruptIf the target's face-down action is Defend, they may reveal it now and raise a shield.
  3. ResolveThe weapon hits the city in your code. You cannot change the target unless a card lets you. If the target has an active shield, the attack is blocked, unless the weapon ignores shields. Otherwise remove the weapon's damage in cubes. Overkill is wasted.
  4. Move the ClockMove it forward by the weapon's Clock value, even if the attack was blocked or hit nothing.
  5. DiscardDiscard the weapon and take your code cards back.

Craters and wrong codes

  • A city with 0 cubes gets a crater token.
  • Hitting a crater makes the rubble bounce. No damage, but the Clock still moves.
  • A wrong code (a number that continent doesn't have, say Asia 5) is a misfire: the weapon fizzles and is discarded without effect, and the Clock does not move. You can't re-aim it.

Fallout

A nuke is any weapon with a Clock value of +1 or more. When a nuke removes cubes from a city, put 1 fallout token on that city's rightmost empty spot. That spot can never hold a cube again. A MIRV or Fallout Bomb leaves fallout on every city it removes cubes from; the Dirty Bomb leaves 3.

Read a city box as one line of spots, solid row first, then dashed. Cubes always fill from the left and fallout always fills from the right, so the first hits on a city take away its room to grow (the dashed row), and once that is gone, fallout eats into the starting spots. A city whose spots are all fallout is gone for good. Conventional weapons (Clock +0) leave no fallout. Reconstruction can remove 1 fallout.

Shields

Every shield blocks an attack completely. Shields differ in what they block: one attack, every attack this round, or one attack sent straight back. See the Build Deck tab.

Two attackers, one Missile Defence

Missile Defence stops only the first attack. If two players hit the same continent, the second one gets through, even if the defender guessed right.

07

Example: two rounds on your board

You play North America. Your board has six card slots: Launchpad, Action and Shield in the first column, three Upgrade slots in the second.

A. Prepare, then Attack

  1. Round 1, PlanPut Prepare face down in your Action slot. Your other four action cards stay in your hand.
  2. Round 1, your turnFlip Prepare. Put an ICBM from your hand and your launch code, the Europe card and the City 3 card (Paris), face down in your Launchpad slot. Everyone sees something is armed, but not what or where.
  3. Round 1, endThe Clock moves 1 minute. Take Prepare back into your hand.
  4. Round 2, PlanPut Attack face down in your Action slot.
  5. Round 2, your turnFlip Attack, then the code and the ICBM ("Europe 3, Paris!"). Europe may interrupt with a shield. If not, Paris loses 4 cubes and the Clock moves +2. Discard the ICBM and take your code cards back.
If you don't attack in round 2

When you flip any other action, the ICBM fizzles: discard it. A decoy is used exactly this way, on purpose.

B. Defending

  1. Round 1An opponent prepares. A card appears on their Launchpad.
  2. Round 2, PlanYou hold a shield, so you put Defend face down in your Action slot.
  3. Round 2, attacker firstThey flip Attack and their code: "North America 1!" You flip Defend immediately, out of turn, and put a Missile Defence from your hand in your Shield slot. The attack is blocked and the Missile Defence is discarded. Your Defend is used up.
  4. Round 2, you firstOn your turn, flip Defend and put the shield in your Shield slot. It protects your whole continent for the rest of the round.
  5. Round 2, endDiscard any shield still in your Shield slot. Take Defend back into your hand. If nobody attacked you, that shield and that turn were wasted.

08

The Doomsday Clock

The Clock counts minutes to midnight. It moves 1 minute every round, plus the Clock value of every weapon launched.

Half an hour to midnight. Starting positions: 30 for 4 players · 27 for 3 · 22 for 2. Red square is midnight.

Some Build cards turn the Clock back, such as Peace Treaty (5 minutes). The Clock never goes above its starting position.

Conventional weapons have Clock +0. A player who is behind wants a long game and fights with them. The leader wants the game over and reaches for nukes.

A nuke sent back by Return to Sender does not move the Clock again. It's the same bomb.

09

Scoring

Count every cube on your cities, whatever its colour. Most wins.

Tie: the player with the most surviving cities (not craters) wins. If still tied, share the victory and the radiation.

Releases

0.8.3

Slots and silos

  • Board slots: Launchpad, Action and Shield in the first column, three Upgrade slots in the second. The Second Silo slot is gone.
  • Up to 3 upgrades per player. Every upgrade is in the Build deck twice (67 cards). Two copies of the same upgrade add up.
  • Second Silo is now Extra Silo: Prepare may put 1 more card on your Launchpad, so two Extra Silos let you hold 3. All of them fire at the one target as separate attacks.
  • Fizzled cards go to the discard face up. The discard pile is open, and it proves the card was a real weapon or decoy. Sabotage follows the same rule.
  • Prepare: your card and your code cards go down as one face-down stack; the order doesn't matter.
  • Setup starts by putting the game board in the middle; the decks go on their spots on the board, and a marker goes on the Clock.
  • The Final Exchange variant is dropped for now.
  • Every upgrade stacks with its second copy. Early Warning's look is optional. Fallout Shelters now cancels 1 fallout per round on one of your cities (it no longer softens hits). Propaganda HQ is renamed Spin Doctors: draw 1 extra Propaganda card and keep one. Shield Network lets Defend play 1 extra shield card, so each extra block still costs a card.

0.8.2

Code cards

  • Launch codes are cards instead of 16 mm tokens: easier to pick, hold and stack. 3 continent cards and up to 6 city cards per player, one sheet for all four players.
  • Each city card lists only the real cities its number hits on your opponents' continents; each continent card lists that continent's cities. Europe gets no City 6 card, since no opponent has a sixth city.
  • Build card names fit at 11 pt: Propaganda Ministry is now Propaganda HQ, Early Warning Radar Early Warning, Disarmament Treaty Peace Treaty, Hypersonic Missile Hypersonic Nuke, Nuclear Submarine Nuclear Sub, Big Firework Firework. Second Silo and Propaganda HQ got clearer rules text.
  • Build card texts reviewed for clarity. Rule fixes along the way: Fallout Bomb and Carpet Bombing say a missing neighbour isn't hit; Return to Sender fizzles if the attacker has no city with that number; the Nuclear Bomber turning back doesn't move the Clock; Hypersonic Nuke simply ignores shields, like the Bunker Buster; upgrades go in an Upgrade slot.
  • Fallout is a general rule now: every nuke that removes cubes puts a fallout token on the city's rightmost empty spot, which can never hold a cube again. It eats the dashed row first, then the starting spots. Dirty Bomb leaves 3; Reconstruction can remove 1.
  • A shared game board: the Doomsday Clock in the middle, spots for both decks and their discards, and boxes for craters, fallout and the cube supply.
  • Attack: flip the launch code and the weapon together; the order doesn't matter.
  • Action cards are printed: 5 per player in their colour, with one shared back per player.
  • Second Silo fires its two weapons as two separate attacks at the same target (one Missile Defence stops only one).
  • A second Five-Year Plan joins the Build deck (60 cards), so an empty Build deck is less likely.
  • Kept Propaganda tricks stay hidden in your hand.
  • Arms Deal: you choose the card you give; the card you take is still random.
  • Nuclear Bomber turning back may re-aim: take the code back and lay a new one face down (or the same). It still has to launch next round or it fizzles.
  • Tsar Bomba hits for 5 instead of 6 (50 megatons); it is still the strongest single-target weapon. A few nukes now name their yield in the lore.
  • SLBM is now prepared with a launch code like any other card (a launchpad without a code gave it away); when it launches you may ignore the code and pick any city.
  • Decoys always pay: when one fizzles, draw 1 Build card, plus 1 more if anyone revealed Defend while it waited (Weather Balloon: plus 1 per defender). A decoy turn is now about as good as a Build turn instead of a gamble.
  • Hand limit 5 (was 6). It counts Build and Propaganda cards only, not action or launch code cards.
  • No more oceans. A code must name a real city. A wrong code by mistake is a misfire: the weapon fizzles without effect and can't be re-aimed.
  • The code cards lie face down on top of your prepared card, so the Launch Code spot on the boards is gone.
  • Helper cards reworked: no footer tag, aligned number badges, bullets beside headings, roomier Targets and fuller sentences.

Dead hand

  • Elimination no longer ends the game. A continent at 0 fires its whole remaining arsenal (launchpad and hand), then its player is out and play continues.
  • The game ends at midnight, when one continent is left (it wins), or when none is left: the Earth is destroyed and everybody loses.

  • When the Clock hits midnight or a continent hits 0, the current round still finishes; then the game ends.
  • Tie: most surviving cities wins.
  • Final Exchange (variant): every weapon still in hand fires, one after another in turn order, no shields.

0.1

First skeleton

  • Doomsday Clock times the game: 1 minute per round plus each weapon's Clock value. Midnight ends the game.
  • Most Population wins. A continent at 0 ends the game at once, so nobody is eliminated and sits out.
  • Population is cubes on numbered cities. Continents keep different city counts (Asia 3, North America 4, Africa 5, Europe 6) with 20 Population each.
  • Rounds: everyone plans in secret, then each player in turn reveals and resolves, starting with the first player.
  • Launch codes commit an exact city (continent + number) during planning. Craters make the rubble bounce, missing numbers land in the ocean. Every player always places a code.
  • Shields fully block and cover the whole continent. Defend can interrupt out of turn when you're attacked.
  • Shield types: Missile Defence (one attack), Iron Curtain (all attacks this round), Return to Sender (sends it back, no extra Clock).
  • Eight weapon types, including spread damage (MIRV, Fallout Bomb), a shield-piercing Bunker Buster, a Bomber that can turn back, and a Clock-free conventional strike.
